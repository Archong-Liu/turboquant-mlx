Background

What is a KV Cache?

When an LLM generates token by token, it computes attention over all previous tokens. Rather than recomputing key and value vectors from scratch every step, models cache them — the KV cache. It's what makes generation fast. It's also what fills up your RAM.

The wall. A 7B model uses ~8 GB of RAM for its weights (4-bit quantized). On a 16 GB Mac, that leaves 8 GB for the KV cache. At fp16, each token costs ~65 KB across all layers — that's your 4,000-token hard ceiling. TurboQuant pushes that ceiling to 20,000+ tokens on the same hardware.
Stage 1

PolarQuant: Quantizing Direction

Attention key vectors in normalized transformers lie on (or near) a unit hypersphere — they all have roughly the same magnitude. PolarQuant exploits this: store the direction cheaply (quantized angle) and the magnitude separately as a single float.

In the interactive demo below, click anywhere inside the circle to place a vector. The green arrow snaps to the nearest quantization bin. Drag the bits slider to see how more bits tighten the approximation.

Stage 2

QJL: Correcting the Residual with 1 Bit

PolarQuant introduces a small error. QJL (Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss) captures that residual using random projections. Each projection compresses one high-dimensional direction down to a single sign bit: +1 or −1.

The Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma guarantees that random projections preserve distances. Even with just sign bits, the reconstructed residual points in roughly the right direction. Below, watch how more projections pull the amber reconstruction closer to the true residual.
